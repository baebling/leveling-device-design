# YAW-A Two-Axis Gimbal Kinematic Definition

Status: Phase 1 preliminary concept proof. Not approved for fabrication.

## Decision

The YAW-A central head is fixed as a **two-axis, yaw-locking yoke**, not as a spherical joint and not as a three-axis gimbal. The axis order is selected deliberately:

```text
O: intersection of the two yoke pin axes and keyed-slide centreline
+X: platform roll axis at level
+Y: fixed lower yoke pitch-pin axis
+Z: keyed-slide translation axis / world vertical at level

Lower yoke: pitch about +Y
Outer carrier: then carries upper roll pin about its local +X
Platform orientation: R = Ry(pitch) * Rx(roll)
```

This order produces world-heading yaw `psi = 0` in the selected `Rz * Ry * Rx` convention throughout the required pitch/roll workspace. A reversed pin order was not adopted because it introduces a coordinate-heading coupling under combined tilt. The mechanism has two revolute freedoms only; a third azimuth/yaw bearing is prohibited.

## Required Constraint Path

```text
lower housing
  -> keyed square guide (Z translation; X/Y and guide yaw constrained)
  -> guide inner head
  -> fixed lower pitch yoke
  -> outer carrier
  -> upper roll yoke
  -> upper platform
```

The yoke transmits the constrained rotational reaction through its two double-shear pin sets. The three actuators provide the commanded pitch/roll position and must be capable of holding the small coupled pitch/roll reaction generated by a world-Z yaw torque at a tilted pose.

The input yaw-torque screen is the active user-confirmed PoC case: 10 kg payload, approximately 10 kg cart, 22 kg moving structure, 0.25 g horizontal acceleration, 50 mm x/y CG offset, design factor 1.5, and torque screen factor 1.5. It produces approximately 16.39 Nm design yaw torque. At 3 degrees pitch, the yoke's locked-axis projection is 0.9986: approximately 16.37 Nm is reacted in the constrained direction and approximately 0.86 Nm couples into the commanded pitch/roll paths. This is a kinematic load-path observation, not a proof of actuator holding or fatigue capacity.

## Stop Interpretation And Correction

The prior `8 deg design / 10 deg hard-stop` values are **total gimbal-tilt envelope values**, not an instruction to permit 10 degrees on both individual pins. Independent 10 degree pin stops would allow about 14.1 degrees at the diagonal pose and violate the existing 10 degree total-tilt limit.

For an easy-to-make PoC, use four adjustable, non-load-bearing stop contacts:

| Stop | Preliminary position | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Pitch + / - | 7 deg each pin | Simple opposing stop screws/shoulders on lower yoke |
| Roll + / - | 7 deg each pin | Simple opposing stop screws/shoulders on upper carrier |
| Worst diagonal total tilt | about 9.89 deg | Stays below the existing 10 deg total-tilt hard-stop envelope |
| Required +/-3 deg diagonal tilt | about 4.24 deg | User-confirmed operating point |
| Required operating tilt plus 2 deg margin | about 6.24 deg | Passes the legacy 8 deg design-clearance target |

The stops are independent of electrical limit switches. They are sacrificial/adjustable physical travel-limit features, not normal operating load supports. Final stop material, contact shape, clearance, energy absorption, fasteners, and attachment are open until CAD and physical evidence.

## Required Physical Mock-Up Before Parameter Approval

1. Use a non-load-bearing wood, plate, or laser-cut gauge with the intended pin diameter, washer stack, and stop locations.
2. Sweep all nine combinations of pitch/roll from -3 to +3 degrees while translating the central guide. There may be no pin, yoke, guide, retention, or actuator-envelope contact.
3. Reach each +/-7 degree individual stop and every diagonal corner. The stop must contact before any unplanned interference; an electrical switch is not a stop.
4. Clamp the guide, apply a hand lever to the platform, and measure independent yaw freeplay. The current candidate limit is no more than 0.5 degrees, with 0.25 degrees preferred.
5. Only after a separate safe bench-fixture plan exists, inspect wear-pad and yoke contact under a representative yaw couple. Do not treat the mock-up as a field-safety or payload test.

## Remaining Risks

- Pin/lug bending, bearing, fatigue, retention, and stop impact capacity are not sized by this kinematic screen.
- Guide wear-pad material, preload/shimming, debris protection, and side-load friction remain open.
- The actuator holding path must be checked separately for the coupled pitch/roll moment at tilted yaw-load cases.
- The actual HRT8E lower rod-end and its bracket need their independent 14 degree no-contact gauge.
